# Crossref feasibility decision

**Recommend B, name-to-ROR resolution with abstention.** The annotations answer which organization received funding credit for a deposited string. In the pair audit, only 4 of 12 same-ID pairs met the stricter requirement that both strings themselves name one organization. The European Union paired with a string crediting both the EU and Greece illustrates the difference. So does the University of Manitoba paired with its graduate fellowship. Direct resolution preserves the operational decision a funding database makes when linking a record to an organization. Ambiguous strings still need explicit exclusions.

**Observed audit.** All 24 pairs are development-exposed, including exclusions. There are 48 distinct source rows and 36 distinct organizations. Each organization occurs in one pair. The rules retained 4 same-ID and 11 different-ID candidates provisionally. Eight same-ID cases were excluded for mixed attribution, award or fund wording, insufficient context, or an embedded identifier. One different-ID case lacked sufficient visible context. For N12 the registry shows a shared US government ancestor, while the agency names and distinct immediate parents establish separate identities. No model outputs entered selection or review. Independent project human adjudications completed: **zero**. [Read all 24 decisions](audits/phase3c/review.md) · [Machine-readable source evidence](audits/phase3c/candidates.json)

**Selection and scope.** Start with one manual ID, zero alternates, active record in the pinned registry, and consistent source labels for a normalized string. Exclude preview rows 0–19. Generate pairs automatically without displaying a holding pool to reviewers. Require at least one complete registry-name field match for positives and two such matches with at least two words each for negatives. This favors recognizable names and understates the difficulty of ordinary incoming negatives. A complete field includes a display name or a whole language group in the CSV. For example, NASA occurs within a multi-acronym field. The field filter is conservative.

Score each pair by Dice similarity between sets of padded character trigrams after NFKC normalization, case folding and joining alphanumeric tokens with spaces. Sample four cases per band from [0,.45), [.45,.72), [.72,.97) for positives and [.50,.65), [.65,.80), [.80,.97) for candidate negatives. Sort within bands by SHA-256 of the selection seed and source row IDs, admitting each organization once. Screen the selected 24 without replacement. Check active status, bidirectional direct relationships of every type, transitive parentage, shared ancestors and identifying words. Registry omissions remain possible. The artificial 12/12 balance makes the audit useful for examining both error directions. Precision depends on match prevalence and false positives, so this sample cannot estimate production precision. The 15 retained cases form a selected development set.

**Prospective experiment for owner review.** The source has 3,505 rows. Of 1,692 singleton rows without alternates, 1,659 map to an active organization in ROR v2.6. Another 1,592 rows have an empty primary list and no alternates. Empty labels express the annotator’s finding in that period. Some no-match cases may be unidentifiable or missing from ROR. Audit their meaning before scoring them. These counts justify a development pilot. A larger study depends on independent label review, candidate coverage and sufficient independent families. [Crossref release and annotation method](https://zenodo.org/records/19699937)

- Target **48 development queries** and later **200 held-out name families**, provisionally 3:1 single-target to audited no-match per split. Count each family once for the primary analysis. Before selecting either set, group shared IDs, normalized names, translations, acronym expansions and repeated award/name families. Group ambiguous neighboring organizations when family overlap would reveal the answer. Put all 48 audited source rows, their organizations and overlapping families on the development side, including excluded rows. Quarantine prior preview rows. Keep ambiguous, multi-target, alternate, identifier-bearing and insufficient-context queries outside the primary set. Freeze methodology and thresholds before accessing held-out cases. Two independent reviewers should label from evidence, record disagreement, and exclude unresolved cases.
- Build candidate lists from a pinned registry using normalized canonical names and aliases. Retrieve five distinct active IDs per query by a fixed lexical method, for example maximum alias trigram Dice. Break ties by stable ID. Freeze the list and its order per query across policies. Never insert the gold ID or use the answer to choose distractors. Show the same names, aliases and country fields to both decision methods. Return one in-list candidate or abstain. Baseline confidence can use the top score and its margin over the runner-up. Fit acceptance cutoffs only on development labels, then freeze them. Compute escalation priority from **visible** scores and margins without reference answers. Handle exact collisions and ties deterministically.
- Compare 0/25/50/100% escalation, with the most uncertain cases first. Freeze one model and prompt. Acquire at most one prediction per query and replay it across policies. Report source-target retrieval recall@5, overall correct resolution, conditional selection accuracy when the target was offered, false links, missed links, correct abstentions, invalid output and service failures. If retrieval misses a true target, abstention is a safe operational decision and an end-to-end miss. Preserve all cases and predeclare baseline fallback on failures. Report coverage, precision/recall/F1 for asserted links, latency, known/unknown cost and paired improvement per additional dollar. Compare within case and summarize uncertainty at family level. Report study proportions separately from any deployment-weighted estimate.
- Allow roughly 1–2 engineering days for an offline development implementation using the shared acquisition, budget and record interfaces. The product prompt, feature weights and binary-only replay require experiment-specific adaptation. Budget 2–4 minutes **per query per reviewer**, plus disagreements, about 4–8 reviewer-hours for 48 cases and 16–32 hours for 200 including adjudication. These are planning estimates.
- As a cost illustration, 48 unique requests at 600 uncached input and 100 output tokens each would cost **$0.00528** at $0.10/$0.50 per million. An illustrative reserve of 2,000 input tokens at the $0.25/M maximum documented short-context write rate plus 4,096 output tokens per call gives **$0.122304** for 48. A proposed $0.15 ceiling would require frozen body-based preflight and separate approval. Model latency, actual token use and quality gains remain unmeasured.
